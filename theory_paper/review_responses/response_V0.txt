RESPONSE TO REFEREE A
================================================================================

We thank the referee for the positive assessment of our manuscript and for the
constructive suggestions on novelty, robustness, and experimental realism. We have
revised the manuscript accordingly and respond to each point below.


1. Robustness to more realistic cracks (unequal intensities, background light, surface
   roughness, aberrations, imperfect mode sorting), and whether the favourable delta z^2
   scaling survives beyond the toy model.

1a. Imperfect mode sorting. We have added a new Supplemental Material section, "Impact of
Mode-Sorting Imperfections on the Classical Fisher Information." There we model the
dominant imperfection of a multi-plane light-converter sorter, namely nearest-neighbour
cross-talk between adjacent Hermite-Gaussian orders, by coupling adjacent modes with a
lossless beam-splitter rotation U(epsilon) before computing the detection probabilities,
their derivatives, and the Fisher information. Because U(epsilon) is orthogonal, the
mixed modes remain a complete orthonormal measurement, and the entire derivation carries
over unchanged. We find that cross-talk preferentially degrades estimation precisely
because the information is concentrated in a few modes; nonetheless, for cross-talk up to
-20 dB (which is experimentally attainable), the Fisher information is reduced but does
not collapse and stays above the direct-imaging benchmark. Mode sorting therefore retains
its advantage over direct imaging across the sub-Rayleigh regime (new figure in the
Supplemental Material).

1b. Aberrated and non-Gaussian PSFs. Our bounds depend only on the Gram matrix of the
PSFs and their parameter derivatives (the closed-form quantum Fisher information matrix
and the reduced Lyapunov equation). The Gaussian assumption enters only through the
closed-form overlaps and the choice of a matched Hermite-Gaussian basis. For an Airy,
aberrated, or experimentally measured PSF, the same expressions hold with the overlaps
evaluated numerically. We now state this explicitly in the Conclusions.

1c. Survival of the delta z^2 scaling. The quadratic Chernoff scaling is structural
rather than an artefact of the toy model: it originates from the activation of the
second-order channels (2,0) and (0,2), whose probability vanishes for a flat surface
(null hypothesis) but grows as delta z^2 for a crack. This mechanism does not rely on the
equal-intensity assumption.

1d. Background light and surface roughness. A detector/background noise floor is treated
in point 4 below.

2. Novelty relative to previous work on quantum superresolution and quantum surface
   metrology, especially Refs. [11] and [12].

We have sharpened the statement of novelty in the Introduction. Relative to Ref. [11],
which develops general expressions for the quantum Fisher information matrix in discrete
quantum imaging, and Ref. [12], which treats the quantum estimation of angular and axial
separations in surface metrology, the present work is new in three respects: (a) it
formulates passive defect detection as a quantum hypothesis test and derives the
governing Chernoff-information bounds for the quantum limit, direct imaging, and mode
sorting, a task not addressed in Refs. [11,12]; (b) it treats the joint estimation of
crack width and depth from the geometry of the point-spread-function manifold, rather
than a single separation parameter; and (c) it provides a closed-form quantum Fisher
information matrix expressed entirely through point-spread-function overlaps and their
derivative couplings, which directly identifies the optimal measurement modes. The
Introduction now states points (a)-(c) explicitly.


3. Number of Hermite-Gaussian modes used, and sensitivity to mode truncation, mode
   mismatch, loss, and detector noise.

3a. Number of modes and truncation. The parameter information is concentrated in the
lowest Hermite-Gaussian orders identified by the QFIM geometry: the (1,0) mode for the
width and the (2,0) and (0,2) modes for the depth. Our numerical results therefore use a
deliberately minimal mode-sorting strategy that resolves only these four modes [(0,0),
(1,0), (2,0), (0,2)] together with a remainder channel collecting all unresolved light
(point 3d). We verify insensitivity to truncation explicitly by comparing this minimal
bank against progressively larger banks (box cutoffs of orders <=2 and <=5): the
Cramer-Rao bounds are indistinguishable in the sub-Rayleigh regime and differ only at
larger separations, where direct imaging is already preferable (new figure in the
Supplemental Material).

3b. Mode mismatch. Mode mismatch and imperfect sorting are addressed by the new
nearest-neighbour cross-talk analysis described in point 1a above.

3c. Loss. A uniform optical/detector inefficiency thins the Poisson process and
rescales every channel mean by the same factor, i.e. it rescales the detection rate
tau. It scales the Fisher information overall but does not change the mode-sorting-
versus-direct-imaging comparison, since both schemes are rescaled equally. We now state
this explicitly in the manuscript. This benign rescaling is distinct from a genuine
detector noise floor (dark counts/background), which is treated in point 4 below.

3d. Remainder channel and higher-order cut-off. We have added a Supplemental Material
section, "The remainder channel," that treats mode truncation explicitly. A finite
sorter resolves only a finite set of Hermite-Gaussian modes; the unresolved light is not
discarded but collected in a single additional output, the dump port of a real device,
which completes the measurement to a valid POVM that accounts for every photon arriving
in the image plane. We now include this remainder channel in the estimation Fisher
information. Because the classical Fisher information is additive over outcomes, and
because merging the unresolved modes into one outcome is a coarse-graining of the fully
resolved measurement, the truncated, remainder-completed, fully resolved, and quantum
bounds obey J_res <= J_res+rem <= J_full <= H. We report two complementary quantities:
(i) the leaked fraction 1 - sum_{j,l} P_{j,l}, i.e. the fraction of collected light not
routed into a resolved mode, and (ii) the Fisher information recovered by the remainder
channel. For the minimal four-mode bank, the resolved probabilities sum to unity through
second order, so the leaked fraction is O(delta^4) in the sub-Rayleigh regime: the
minimal bank captures essentially all photons and all parameter information. Enlarging
the bank reduces the leaked fraction but improves the estimation precision only near and
beyond the Rayleigh length, where direct imaging already attains the quantum limit.
Truncation to a handful of modes plus a remainder channel therefore costs negligible
information throughout the regime where mode sorting is advantageous (new figures in the
Supplemental Material). We emphasise that this measurement-design leakage is distinct
from uniform optical/detector loss (point 3c), which rescales all channel probabilities
equally and does not affect the mode-sorting-versus-direct-imaging comparison.

We thank the referee for carefully reading our manuscript and providing constructive
feedback that helped us improve the paper. We have revised the manuscript and added
explanations to address each question. We hope the referee will recommend the paper for
publication with these changes.


1. Can the formalism be extended beyond Gaussian PSFs to other PSFs such as the Airy
   disk, aberrated, or experimentally measured PSFs?

The framework is PSF-agnostic. The quantum Fisher information matrix and the reduced
Lyapunov equation depend only on the Gram matrix of the PSFs and their parameter
derivatives. The Gaussian assumption enters only through the closed-form overlaps given
in the Supplemental Material and through the choice of a matched Hermite-Gaussian mode
basis; for an Airy, aberrated, or experimentally measured PSF, the same expressions hold
with the overlaps evaluated numerically. We have clarified this in the Conclusions of the
manuscript.


2. Please clarify the meaning of "axial defocus" while depth information is concentrated
   in second-order modes.

By "axial defocus" we mean the quadratic wavefront curvature induced by an axial (z)
displacement of a point source. The leading tangent direction generated by such a
displacement is proportional to the second-order transverse derivative of the PSF, which
for a Gaussian PSF is the combination of the (2,0) and (0,2) Hermite-Gaussian modes. This
is why the depth information concentrates in second-order modes. We have added this
clarification to the main text.

   intensities, and does mode sorting remain optimal in the sub-Rayleigh regime?

4. What is the intuition behind the optimality of Hermite-Gaussian modes in the
   sub-Rayleigh regime?

For a Gaussian PSF, the tangent vectors generated by infinitesimal source displacements
are proportional to Hermite-Gaussian functions of increasing order. A Hermite-Gaussian
sorter therefore projects directly onto the information-bearing directions of the PSF
manifold. In the sub-Rayleigh limit a single low order dominates each parameter (first
order for the width, second order for the depth), so a small number of modes already
saturates the quantum limit. We have made this geometric intuition explicit in the
manuscript.


5. The possible future explorations are not clearly stated, and the generality and
   experimental feasibility could be strengthened.

On generality, the framework is PSF-agnostic (point 1): because the bounds depend only on
PSF overlaps and their derivatives, they apply unchanged to non-Gaussian, aberrated, or
experimentally measured PSFs, for which the overlaps are evaluated numerically. We now
state this explicitly in the Conclusions.

On experimental feasibility, we have added two Supplemental Material sections. (i) The
section "Impact of Mode-Sorting Imperfections on the Classical Fisher Information" models
nearest-neighbour cross-talk of a multi-plane light-converter sorter and shows that the
mode-sorting advantage survives realistic, imperfect sorting (it remains above the
direct-imaging benchmark for cross-talk up to -20 dB). (ii) The section "The remainder
channel" models the finite mode bank of a realistic sorter together with its dump-port
(remainder) channel, and shows that resolving only the few information-bearing modes plus
this single extra outcome -- which together account for every photon in the image plane
-- suffices to reach the quantum limit in the sub-Rayleigh regime, so the protocol does
not require an unbounded mode bank. Together these motivate a proof-of-principle
demonstration, which we now highlight in the Conclusions.

revision (see also our response to Referee A, point 4):
