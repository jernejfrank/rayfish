Dear Editor,

Thank you for considering our work for publication in PRL. Both referees find the
paper of interest and have asked for reasonable additional material, which has
strengthened the manuscript. We have addressed every point in the self-contained
responses below and in the resubmitted manuscript and Supplemental Material (SM).

novelty and made the geometric intuition behind the Hermite-Gaussian modes explicit.

PART 2 - RESPONSE TO REFEREE 1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We thank the referee for the positive assessment and for the constructive suggestions
on robustness, novelty, and experimental realism. We respond to each point below; full
derivations and figures are in the revised manuscript and Supplemental Material.


POINT 1. Robustness to more realistic cracks (unequal intensities, background light,
surface roughness, aberrations, imperfect mode sorting), and whether the delta z^2
scaling survives.

Our bounds depend only on the PSF overlaps and their derivatives, so most of these are
accommodated without changing the framework. Briefly:

(a) Unequal brightness -- new supplemental section "Robustness to unequal
source brightness." A brightness imbalance only reallocates the fixed photon budget
between width and depth; the mode-sorting-to-quantum efficiency stays flat and close to
unity sub-Rayleigh and is essentially independent of the weights, so the result holds
whether the intensities are known, unknown, or fluctuating.

(b) Background light -- new supplemental subsection "Detector noise floor." A noise floor erodes the fainter depth channels before the width channel, so depth degrades first, but the effect stays moderate up to percent-level background.

(c) Aberrations / non-Gaussian PSFs -- the framework is PSF-agnostic; for an Airy,
aberrated, or measured PSF the same expressions hold with the overlaps evaluated
numerically (now stated in the Conclusions).

(d) Imperfect mode sorting -- survives realistic cross-talk; see Point 3(b).

(e) Surface roughness -- we flag a quantitative treatment as future work.

(f) delta z^2 scaling -- structural: it comes from the activation of the (2,0)/(0,2)
channels (occupancy ~ delta z^2), which a per-source weight only multiplies; it
therefore survives unequal brightness, as confirmed in (a).


POINT 2. Novelty relative to previous work, especially Refs. [11] and [12].

The Introduction now states three new contributions relative to Refs. [11,12]: (a) it
casts passive defect detection as a quantum hypothesis test and derives the governing
Chernoff bounds; (b) it treats the joint estimation of crack width and depth from the
PSF-manifold geometry, not a single separation; and (c) it gives a closed-form quantum
Fisher information matrix in terms of PSF overlaps that directly identifies the optimal
modes.


POINT 3. Number of Hermite-Gaussian modes, and sensitivity to truncation, mode
mismatch, loss, and detector noise.

(a) Modes and truncation -- new supplemental section "The remainder channel." We resolve only the four information-bearing modes [(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(0,2)] plus a remainder channel; the leaked fraction is O(delta^4) sub-Rayleigh and larger modes are indistinguishable from this minimal one in that regime.

(b) Mode mismatch / imperfect sorting -- new supplemental section "Mode-Sorting Bounds under Experimental Imperfections." Modelling nearest-neighbour cross-talk, the mode-sorting advantage stays above direct imaging up to -20 dB cross-talk.

(c) Loss -- a uniform inefficiency only rescales the rate tau and cancels in the
mode-sorting-versus-direct-imaging comparison (now stated in the manuscript).

(d) Detector noise -- treated under Point 1(b).

PART 3 - RESPONSE TO REFEREE 2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We thank the referee for the careful reading and the constructive questions, which have
improved the paper. We respond to each below; details are in the revised manuscript and
Supplemental Material.


QUESTION 1. Can the formalism be extended beyond Gaussian PSFs (Airy, aberrated, or
measured)?

Yes -- the framework is PSF-agnostic: the bounds depend only on the PSF overlaps and
their derivatives. The Gaussian assumption enters only through the closed-form overlaps
and the matched Hermite-Gaussian basis; for any other PSF the same expressions hold with
the overlaps evaluated numerically (now stated in the Conclusions).


QUESTION 2. Clarify "axial defocus" given that depth information sits in second-order
modes.

"Axial defocus" is the quadratic wavefront curvature from an axial (z) displacement. Its
leading tangent direction is the second-order transverse derivative of the PSF, which
for a Gaussian PSF is the (2,0)+(0,2) combination -- hence depth information concentrates in second-order modes (now clarified in the main text).


QUESTION 3. Robustness to unknown or fluctuating intensities; does mode sorting remain
optimal sub-Rayleigh?

New supplemental section "Robustness to unequal source brightness." A brightness imbalance only reallocates the fixed photon budget between width and depth, and the
mode-sorting-to-quantum efficiency stays flat and close to unity sub-Rayleigh. Because
this efficiency is essentially independent of the weights, the conclusion holds whether
the intensities are fluctuating: mode sorting remains near-optimal in
the sub-Rayleigh regime.


QUESTION 4. Intuition for the optimality of Hermite-Gaussian modes sub-Rayleigh.

The tangent vectors generated by infinitesimal source displacements are proportional to
Hermite-Gaussian functions of increasing order, so a Hermite-Gaussian sorter projects
directly onto the information-bearing directions of the PSF manifold. Sub-Rayleigh, one
low order dominates each parameter (first for width, second for depth), so a few modes
already saturate the quantum limit (now made explicit in the manuscript).


QUESTION 5. Future explorations, generality, and experimental feasibility.

Generality: the PSF-agnostic framework (Question 1) applies to non-Gaussian, aberrated,
or measured PSFs, and extends to continuous emitter distributions -- the natural route to surface roughness, which we flag as future work in the Conclusions.

Feasibility: new supplemental sections show the scheme works with a finite, realistic sorter (the remainder/dump-port channel reaches the quantum limit with a handful of modes), survives imperfect sorting (cross-talk up to -20 dB), tolerates a percent-level detector noise floor, and is robust to unequal source brightness (Question 3). Together these motivate a proof-of-principle demonstration, now highlighted in the Conclusions.


We hope these amendments address both referees' concerns and that they will recommend
our work for publication.
